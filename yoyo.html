How it started

A driveway, a Duncan, and way too much free time

I got my first yoyo in elementary school, the cheap plastic kind that barely slept for three seconds. It didn't matter — I was hooked instantly. Over the next few years I traded up through fixed axles, then transaxles, then my first real ball-bearing throw, and every upgrade unlocked a new layer of tricks I hadn't known were possible.

These days my go-to throw is a gold MAGICYOYO N8 — still smoother and longer-sleeping than anything I owned as a kid.

What kept me coming back wasn't really the tricks themselves. It was the community. Local yoyo clubs, meetups, and eventually a small competitive scene turned a solo backyard hobby into something social — trading tips, filming combos, and cheering each other on when a trick finally landed clean.

"The best part of yoyoing was never the trick. It was the ten friends who showed up just to watch you land it."

The trick guide

Four families of yoyo tricks

Every yoyo trick I ever learned falls into one of these four broad categories, roughly in the order most players learn them.

  1. Looping

    Tricks performed with the yoyo staying "on string" and returning to the hand between throws.

    • Forward Pass
    • Breakaway
    • Around the Corner
    • Loop the Loop two-handed

  2. String Tricks

    Tricks built on the sleeper, where the yoyo spins at the end of the string while you weave patterns around it.

    • Sleeper
    • Trapeze
    • Around the World
    • Rock the Baby
    • Elevator intermediate

  3. Offstring

    The yoyo is completely detached from the string mid-trick and caught again, closer to juggling than spinning.

    • Basic Toss and Catch
    • Chopsticks Catch
    • Whip Catch advanced

  4. Freehand

    A counterweight replaces the player's finger, letting both the yoyo and the counterweight fly independently.

    • Iced Latte
    • Skyrocket
    • Counterweight Grind advanced
